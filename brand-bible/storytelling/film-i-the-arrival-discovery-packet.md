## Story Trust Preface

This packet treats the **approved concept** as **raw ore**, not finished design. Story Trust’s job is to **pressure-test** emotional logic, **protect mystery**, **reject beauty-ad cliché**, and **elevate** the film from “elegant brand film” to **franchise opening chapter**.

**Verdict on concept spine (provisional):** The **CLOSED → OPEN** gesture reframed as **guest arrival** (not store hours) is **strong, ownable hospitality mythology**—worthy of protection. The **storefront discovery** frame is **serviceable but risky** if it reads as **literal retail** or **cast protagonist** that narrows identification.

**Highest-priority discovery work before script:**

1. Resolve **protagonist grammar** (who is “the woman” in relation to the Slayer?).  
2. Ensure **world scale** exceeds **single shop** without exposition.  
3. Engineer **Film II hunger** in the **final emotional beat**, not only plot tease.  
4. Audit every story beat for **product/tech/business leakage**.

## Film Purpose

**Primary purpose:** Introduce **the world** of Frontal Slayer as a **somewhere**—a luxury experiential property the audience has **not** visited before.

| Film I must accomplish | Film I must not accomplish |
| --- | --- |
| Establish **tone, light, behavior, scale-hint** | Explain **catalog, app, membership SKUs** |
| Create **one unforgettable hospitality truth** | Close the **dramatic question** |
| Seed **guide (PSA)** as host, not seller | Position PSA as **influencer** |
| Leave **“What is Frontal Slayer?”** alive | Answer **“What do they sell?”** as headline |
| Make **Film II feel necessary** | Feel **self-contained ad** |

**Alignment:** Matches Chapter One constitution—*first handshake, not store opening*.

## Core Theme

**Theme:** **You were expected.**

Not as marketing flattery—as **world law**. In Frontal Slayer’s universe, arrival is **reciprocal**: the guest chooses the threshold; the house **responds**. The theme rejects transactional beauty and replaces it with **recognition without familiarity** (they do not know the place yet, but the place behaves as if **dignity was reserved**).

**Secondary theme:** **Doors are decisions.** Every threshold is identity—crossing is **agency**, not **compliance**.

---

## Emotional Thesis

> *Luxury is not sold here—it is **offered** when someone brave enough to pause finally steps forward.*

The film argues emotionally that **pause** ( noticing, hesitating, choosing ) is **worthy of ceremony**. PSA’s sign flip is not operational—it is **acknowledgment** of that pause.

## Central Dramatic Question

**Hero question (locked with Chapter One bible):**

> **What is Frontal Slayer—and why does it feel like somewhere instead of something?**

**Film I sub-question (for discovery):**

> **Why did the world **open** for *this* arrival—and what exists beyond the first room?**

The sub-question **must** remain **partially unanswered** at fade-out.

## Audience Curiosity Journey

| Phase | Curiosity state | Design intent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Open** | Low / guarded | Audience expects **ad**; film must **break pattern** in first beats (pace, sound, no logo slap) |
| **Notice** | Spike | Something **architectural or sonic** is “wrong” in a good way—too beautiful, too calm |
| **Pause** | Held | Protagonist pause **mirrors** viewer hesitation—sync identification |
| **Threshold** | Acceleration | Curiosity **pulls forward**—what’s inside? |
| **Reveal (partial)** | Depth without map | Glimpses **raise** room count in imagination |
| **Close** | **One sharp question** | Not “what happened?” but **“what is this place?”** + **“what happens when trust deepens?”** (Film II) |

**Story Trust note:** Curiosity must **net increase**. If we **answer** “it’s a salon/store,” curiosity **collapses**.

The film **promises**:

1. This brand operates like a **host**, not a **vendor**.  
2. There is **more space** than we are shown.  
3. A **guide** exists who **protects standards**.  
4. Beauty here is tied to **craft and identity**, not **fixing**.

**Implicit contract:** We will **not** insult your intelligence with exposition or hard sell in Film I.

## Character Intentions

### Guest (on-screen protagonist figure)

**Surface intention:** See what the place is; satisfy curiosity **safely**.  
**Deep intention:** Be **seen** without being **judged**; test if luxury will **include** her.  
**Unspoken fear:** Another beauty space that **shrinks** her or **pushes** her.  
**End intention:** Stay **open**—cross threshold **psychologically**, not only physically.

**Story Trust flag — PROTAGONIST GRAMMAR:**  
Canon says the **Slayer** is every member; default is **POV / hands / silhouette**, not **cast lead**. Current concept centers **“a woman.”**

| Option | Strength | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A — Cast woman as Slayer stand-in** | Clear emotional performance | Narrows who belongs; beauty-ad trope |
| **B — POV / partial figure only** | Maximum identification | Harder performance read for sign beat |
| **C — Hybrid:** distant figure + **heavy POV entry** | Best of both | Requires disciplined edit |

**Recommendation:** **C — Hybrid.** Exterior: we **sense** a woman (silhouette, back, reflection). Interior entry: **POV** or **OTS** dominates so audience **becomes** guest. PSA’s sign beat plays to **arrival**, not **character study**.

### PSA

**Surface intention:** Welcome the guest properly.  
**Deep intention:** **Activate** the house for **this** person—fulfill host role.  
**Hidden intention (see below):** Confirm the guest is **ready for first threshold**—not sell.

**Performance intent:** Calm **authority** + **warmth**; movement **unhurried**; eye line **respect**, not **scan for wallet**.

## PSA's Hidden Objective

**Not secret evil—secret host logic.**

PSA’s hidden objective: **The house must behave correctly for a first arrival**—because **first impressions become canon** in the member’s memory.

She is not “opening shop.” She is **changing state** of the world from **waiting** to **present**. Subtext: *We do not perform for foot traffic. We **receive** people.*

**Avoid misread:** PSA as **gatekeeper** who **judges** guest worthiness. Worthiness is **not** the theme—**recognition** is.

## Potential Weaknesses

### Weakness 1 — “Woman discovers store” is familiar

**Problem:** Reads like **fashion film / perfume ad / flagship opening**.  
**Fix:** Emphasize **response of world** (sign, light, sound) over **shopping desire**. Add **non-retail depth cue**. Hybrid **POV**.

### Weakness 2 — Storefront limits universe scale

**Problem:** Audience cages brand as **single location**.  
**Fix:** Architecture **implies** continuation—corridor, stair, **second light source**, **Lounge glow**.

### Weakness 3 — PSA “notices her” can feel creepy

**Problem:** Luxury surveillance trope.  
**Fix:** PSA notices **from host station**, calm **approach**, **no rush**—**ritual** visible through glass **before** guest commits.

### Weakness 4 — Glimpses without grammar

**Problem:** Random pretty shots = **montage ad**.  
**Fix:** Glimpses follow **one vector**—guest **movement path** reveals **layers** in order: entry → public craft → **hint private**.

### Weakness 5 — Weak Film II bridge

**Problem:** End on **beauty smile** = done.  
**Fix:** End on **unresolved trust need**—guest **wants** to understand **standards**, not **price**.

### Weakness 6 — Dialogue temptation

**Problem:** Writers may **explain** sign.  
**Fix:** Lock discovery: **zero exposition** in Film I script phase.
