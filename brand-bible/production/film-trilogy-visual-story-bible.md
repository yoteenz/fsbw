## THE STORY

This is **NOT** the story of someone searching for Frontal Slayer.

This is the story of someone enjoying an **ordinary morning**…

…until something **extraordinary** quietly interrupts it.

The boutique **never chases** Nia.

**Nia discovers** the boutique.

The **audience discovers** it with her.

---

## THE EMOTIONAL JOURNEY

**Beginning**

Routine.

Comfort.

Joy.

Music.

Movement.

↓

Curiosity.

↓

Discovery.

↓

Wonder.

↓

Choice.

↓

Transformation.

---

## THE AUDIENCE

The audience should **never** feel like they're watching Nia.

They should feel like they **ARE** Nia.

Every decision should reinforce **relatability**.

Never perfection.

Never performance.

---

## NIA

Late 20s.

Confident.

Naturally stylish.

Comfortable alone.

Lives in her own world.

She notices beautiful things.

She isn't trying to impress anyone.

She simply has **excellent taste**.

---

## NIA'S FINAL LOOK (LOCKED)

### Hair

Long luxurious soft waves.

Healthy.

Voluminous.

Natural movement.

### Wardrobe

- Matching **white cropped fitted polo**  
- Matching **white pleated skirt**  
- Subtle **Frontal Slayer red piping** around the polo  
- Matching **Frontal Slayer red trim** around the skirt hem  
- **White toe shoes** with red detailing  

### Accessories

- Minimal jewelry  
- **White earbuds**  
- **Iced matcha or latte**  
- **One hand remains free at all times**  
- **No purse**  
- **No sunglasses**  

---

## NIA'S PERSONALITY

Throughout the beginning of the film she should naturally:

- Mouth lyrics  
- Lightly dance  
- Walk to the beat  
- Tap her fingers  
- Smile occasionally  
- Sip her drink  
- Look into storefront windows  

Everything should feel **subconscious**.

Never choreographed.

---

## THE BOUTIQUE

Frontal Slayer is **never** introduced dramatically.

It **already exists**.

It has existed **before** the film started.

The boutique should feel **timeless**.

Almost as if it has quietly been **waiting** for the right person to notice it.

---

## DISCOVERY

The audience should discover the boutique **exactly the same way** Nia does.

Nothing should ever be **spoiled**.

Never reveal too much too early.

---

## PACING

Every shot has **one purpose**.

Never combine multiple emotional beats into **one frame**.

Allow every realization to **breathe**.

The audience should have enough time to **process** what just happened.

---

## THE FIRST ACT

### FRAME 001 — THE BOUTIQUE AWAKENS

**Interior.** Morning. Luxury boutique.

The bell gently settles after a customer has already exited.

No people.

Silence.

**Purpose:** Introduce the world.

---

### FRAME 002 — A REFLECTION IN MOTION

Still inside the boutique.

Looking through the storefront glass.

Far away…

A blurry silhouette appears.

We cannot identify her.

She's simply another pedestrian.

She's lightly dancing.

Mouthing lyrics.

Listening to music.

She's completely absorbed in her own world.

A slow subtle push-in begins toward her.

Cut before reaching her.

**Purpose:** The boutique notices Nia before we do.

---

### FRAME 003 — MEET NIA

Cut outside.

Medium side tracking shot.

The storefront is no longer visible.

We simply spend time with Nia.

She is:

- walking  
- mouthing lyrics  
- lightly dancing  
- smiling  
- taking occasional sips  
- enjoying herself  

**Purpose:** Introduce Nia.

---

### FRAME 004 — EVERYDAY BEAUTY

As she walks…

She casually glances into different storefronts.

Bookstore.

Flower shop.

Cafe.

Fashion boutique.

No reaction.

She keeps walking.

**Purpose:** Teach the audience that this is normal behavior for her.

---

### FRAME 005 — THE GLANCE

Without any emphasis…

Frontal Slayer enters frame naturally.

She gives it the **exact same glance**.

Looks forward.

Continues walking.

Still mouthing lyrics.

Still dancing slightly.

**Purpose:** The audience thinks nothing happened.

---

### FRAME 006 — THE BRAIN CATCH-UP

One step.

Two steps.

Her lyric slowly trails off.

Not dramatically.

Just…

"..."

She instinctively removes **ONE** earbud.

She stops walking.

Still looking forward.

**Purpose:** Her brain catches up to what her eyes already saw.

---

### FRAME 007 — THE REWIND

Still looking forward…

She slowly backs up.

One step.

Another.

She **never** looks toward the boutique.

**Purpose:** Build anticipation.

---

### FRAME 008 — THE REALIZATION

Only after she has backed up into position…

She turns.

Now she truly looks.

A beat.

Her mouth opens slightly.

Not exaggerated.

Just genuine curiosity.

**Purpose:** The audience experiences the discovery with her.

---

### FRAME 009 — THE CHOICE

Without looking away…

She removes the **second** earbud.

The music is over.

Her attention belongs somewhere else now.

She takes one intentional step toward Frontal Slayer.

**CUT.**

**Purpose:** Her ordinary morning has officially changed direction.

---

## THE RULE OF MUSIC

Music represents **Nia's world**.

Frontal Slayer **never** interrupts the music.

**Nia chooses** to leave it behind.

| Earbud | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Earbud one** | Subconscious reaction |
| **Earbud two** | Conscious decision |

---

## THE RULE OF COMEDY

Comedy comes from **recognition**.

Not exaggeration.

The audience laughs because they've done the **same thing**.

Never overact.

Never oversell.

---

## THE RULE OF DISCOVERY

The audience should constantly think:

> **"I just noticed that."**

instead of

> **"The movie wanted me to notice that."**

---

## THE FINAL PRINCIPLE

Every frame should answer one question:

> **"What would naturally happen next if this were a real morning?"**

If the answer feels **authentic**…

Keep it.

If it feels cinematic for the sake of being cinematic…

Remove it.

The Frontal Slayer trilogy should feel less like a film…

…and more like **remembering the best morning you never realized would change everything**.
