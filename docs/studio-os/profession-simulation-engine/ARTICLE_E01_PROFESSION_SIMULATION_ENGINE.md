# ARTICLE-E01 — Profession Simulation Engine™

**Status:** Accepted  
**System:** Profession Simulation Engine™  
**Governance layer:** Layer 1 — Design Principles™  
**Principle:** Careers Are Simulated™  
**Graph role:** `profession` · `career-stage` · `simulation-scene` · `W-PRO-*` / `W-CST-*` / `W-SCN-*`  
**Approved:** 2026-07-08  

---

## One sentence

**Studio World does not teach professions as courses; it lets learners enter a living workplace and progress by performing the profession.**

---

## Purpose

Studio World should not make learners feel like they are taking traditional education.

The learner should feel like:

- they clocked in on Day One,
- they met real coworkers,
- they received real clients,
- they made real professional decisions,
- they built a reputation,
- they earned promotion through performance,
- they became the profession by living it.

The **Profession Simulation Engine™** converts knowledge into immersive career progression.

It is the system that turns a **Profession Brain™** into:

- workplace scenes,
- shifts,
- jobs,
- clients,
- challenges,
- projects,
- AI character ecosystems,
- promotion ladders,
- certifications,
- income progression,
- adaptive scenarios.

## Definition

A **Profession Simulation™** is:

- an immersive workplace,
- a career progression system,
- a scenario engine,
- a role-playing learning environment,
- a professional identity path,
- a certification system,
- a memory-bearing AI ecosystem,
- a World Graph citizen.

A Profession Simulation™ is **not**:

- a course,
- a video library,
- a quiz funnel,
- a static LMS,
- a gamified checklist,
- a passive knowledge page.

---

## Relationship to Studio Institute™

Studio Institute™ remains the knowledge development and certification surface.

The Profession Simulation Engine™ changes the primary learner experience.

| System | Role |
|--------|------|
| **Profession Brain™** | Source of professional truth: knowledge, standards, procedures, ethics, scenarios |
| **Studio Institute™** | Publishes learning paths, references, certifications, and knowledge surfaces |
| **Profession Simulation Engine™** | Converts the Profession Brain into playable workplace progression |
| **Skill Graph™** | Tracks mastered skills, prerequisites, certification evidence, and gaps |
| **Professional Profile™** | Records learner identity, reputation, promotions, and career history |
| **Simulation Engine™** | Models business decisions; adjacent but distinct from career simulation |

Studio Institute can still expose reference material, but the default path should be:

```text
Profession Brain™
    ↓
Profession Simulation Engine™
    ↓
Scenes · Shifts · Clients · Projects · Promotions
    ↓
Skill Graph™ · Certifications · Professional Profile™
```

## Core principles

### 1. Careers over courses

Every supported profession should become a playable career.

The learner should not ask, “What lesson am I on?”

They should ask:

- “What shift am I working?”
- “Which client is waiting?”
- “What does my mentor need from me?”
- “What promotion am I preparing for?”

### 2. Workplace over content

Every learning moment occurs inside a workplace scene.

Examples:

- salon floor,
- photo studio,
- construction site,
- finance office,
- commercial kitchen,
- legal intake room,
- hospital unit,
- film set,
- architecture studio,
- marketing war room.

### 3. Performance over consumption

Learners progress by doing the work:

- diagnosing,
- deciding,
- communicating,
- repairing,
- producing,
- documenting,
- serving clients,
- managing risk,
- handling unexpected events.

Passive content may exist as reference, but it must not become the primary experience.

### 4. Characters remember

AI characters inside a profession simulation must remember previous interactions and adapt over time.

Character types include:

- Mentor™,
- Manager™,
- Coworkers™,
- Clients™,
- Inspectors™,
- Suppliers™,
- Students™,
- Competitors™.

The learner’s reputation should affect how characters respond.

### 5. Promotion unlocks the world

Every career stage unlocks:

- new responsibilities,
- new clients,
- new environments,
- new tools,
- new income,
- new AI scenarios,
- new certifications.

Promotion is not only a badge. It expands the simulated profession.

## Career progression model

Every career stage should define:

- title,
- stage order,
- workplace environment,
- responsibilities,
- unlocked tools,
- client types,
- shift types,
- challenges,
- certification requirements,
- promotion gates,
- income range,
- reputation expectations,

### Promotion gates

Promotion should be earned from evidence:

- skill completion,
- scenario performance,
- mentor approval,
- client satisfaction,
- safety compliance,
- mistake recovery,
- project completion,
- certification pass,
- reputation threshold.

## World Graph citizenship

E01 introduces three World Graph node types:

| Node type | Prefix | Purpose |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| `profession` | `W-PRO-*` | A profession Studio World can simulate |
| `career-stage` | `W-CST-*` | A promotion stage within a profession |
| `simulation-scene` | `W-SCN-*` | A playable workplace scene / shift environment |

Relationships:

- Profession Simulation Engine™ **owns** professions.
- Professions **own** career stages.
- Career stages **unlock** simulation scenes (represented by `projects-to` until a dedicated unlock edge exists).
- Profession Brain™ **feeds** the simulation engine.
- Skill Graph™ and Professional Profile™ **record** evidence and progression.
- Studio Institute™ **publishes** reference/certification surfaces derived from simulation outcomes.

## Forbidden implementation pattern

Do not build a profession as:

- a course module list,
- a video playlist,
- static quiz pages,
- generic LMS cards,
- isolated lessons without workplace context,
- gamified badges disconnected from career responsibility.

If an experience can be renamed “Lesson 3” without losing meaning, it has failed E01.

---

## Success criteria

Studio World becomes an immersive career simulator where learners master professions by living them.

The learner should feel:

> “I am becoming this professional.”

Not:

> “I am taking a course about this profession.”
