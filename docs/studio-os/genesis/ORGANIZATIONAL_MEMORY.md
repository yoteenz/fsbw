# Organizational Memory™

**Genesis article:** [`../../../genesis/articles/ORGANIZATIONAL_MEMORY.md`](../../../genesis/articles/ORGANIZATIONAL_MEMORY.md)  
**Status:** Constitutional amendment proposal for Genesis review

## Decision

Organizational Memory™ is approved as a constitutional capability—not a peer
engine or an independent memory database.

It is realized through:

```text
native outcome systems
  + Institute evidence and knowledge
  + Company Genome Organizational Learning Ledger™
  + Studio Intelligence retrieval and synthesis
```

## Ownership

- Native systems own facts and outcome records.
- Institute of Knowledge™ owns evidence corpus and reviewed knowledge.
- Company Genome™ owns retained organization-specific Experience Cases™,
  Learned Patterns™, and Wisdom Candidates™ in the Organizational Learning
  Ledger™.
- Studio Intelligence™ owns pattern/forecast/recommendation synthesis.
- Memory Briefs™ are read-only contextual projections, not a memory registry.

## Learning distinctions

| Statement | Class |
|---|---|
| “This happened.” | Source fact / Experience Case™ |
| “This usually happens under these conditions.” | Scoped Learned Pattern™ |
| “This is likely to happen again.” | Forecast with assumptions and calibration |
| “We should do this.” | Recommendation or governed Decision™ |

Facts do not decay; applicability does. Patterns, forecasts, and wisdom
candidates require confidence, scope, counterexamples, recency/review, and
revalidation. Organizational forgetting deprecates or restricts operational
influence without erasing needed provenance.

## Consumer boundaries

Studio Intelligence may recommend repeating a strategy from prior outcomes, but
cannot promote it to organizational truth. Workflow, Attention, Orb,
Headquarters, and Experience systems may consume bounded Memory Briefs™; none
may automatically change behavior from historical learning.

See the Genesis article for retention classes, lifecycle, contradiction handling,
inheritance, ten-year scaling, and governance rules.
