# Guided Apprenticeship

Studio World can teach a virtual guide how a company actually works. All In One is the first practice case. The guide is not a new product name, and no character has been cast.

## The experience

Show me how you think it works.

No. Let me show you.

Got it. Let me try your way.

Yes. That’s how we do it here.

The guide demonstrates a prepared process in a practice office. The expert can interrupt, take the desk, and show a different order. The guide repeats only what was demonstrated and explained. The expert confirms. The owner approves. Team training is a later, separate permission. A worker still cannot file, pay, or change a live account.

## One story

A fictional company, Northline Hauling, needs operating-authority help.

The guide starts with: collect information, prepare the application, verify details, review and submit.

The expert says verification happens before preparation, and says why. The guide replays that order. The first order is kept as the researched baseline. The expert’s sentence is kept beside it.

The owner can see the result. That does not publish training and does not authorize a worker.

## Approval and safety

A click is not a reason. A reason is not expert confirmation. Confirmation is not owner approval. Approval is not a published lesson. A published lesson is not permission to use a tool. Practice is not a live filing.

Another company’s reader cannot open the lesson. A staff trainee does not see restricted expert notes. An AI teammate gets no tools from finishing training.

If the expert’s way would drop a legal step, the system flags it and does not delete the step.

If the demonstration shows checks split across screens, that becomes a design request. It does not change the office.

## What this proof is
