# Organizational Attention™

**Project:** Studio OS  
**System:** Organizational Attention™  
**Status:** Constitutional amendment proposal for Genesis review  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Authority:** Genesis.md  
**Extends:** Universal Decision Architecture™, Company Genome™, Company Operating
Manual™, Studio Intelligence Layer™, Orb™, Workflow Engine™, Executive
Headquarters™  
**Decision:** Approve Organizational Attention™ as the evolution of Notification
Engine™ into constitutional attention orchestration. It is not a Company
Genome™ capability, a peer source of organization truth, or a second decision
engine.

---

## 0. Constitutional decision

Studio OS needs a permanent answer to:

> Given finite executive capacity, what deserves attention now, in what form,
> and what can safely wait?

The proposal is correct about the missing capability but incorrect if placed
inside Company Genome™.

Company Genome™ owns enduring organizational meaning, constraints, goals,
topology, and learning. Organizational attention is a dynamic, time-bound
allocation decision made from that context. Placing it in the Genome would
confuse what the company **is** with what a specific executive should see
**now**.

Existing Notification Engine™ already owns notification timing, delivery,
preference, and learning, and explicitly evolves toward attention
orchestration. This amendment therefore:

```text
Notification Engine™
  -> evolves into Organizational Attention™
    -> owns attention orchestration
      -> contains Attention Delivery™
```

Notification delivery becomes a subsystem, not the constitutional purpose.

---

## 1. Prime law

```text
Attention is scarce.
Truth does not automatically deserve interruption.
Urgency does not automatically deserve executive action.
No signal becomes executive attention without context, comparison, and a
defined next action or reason to observe.
```

The objective is not maximum notification engagement. It is better executive
judgment, protected focus, timely intervention, and accountable follow-through.

## 2. System definition

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Official Name™** | Organizational Attention™ |
| **Purpose** | Allocate finite organizational and executive attention to the most consequential, actionable, and appropriately timed matters. |

| **Primary users** | Founder, executive team, department leads, AI workforce, Orb™, Executive Headquarters™. |
| **Core principle** | Attention is an allocation and routing decision, never a raw event feed. |

## 3. Ownership and boundaries

### 3.1 Organizational Attention™ owns

- candidate normalization and deduplication
- comparative attention assessment
- attention budgets, timing, batching, and interruption policy
- audience/role routing
- briefing, digest, delegation, suppression, and escalation routing decisions
- delivery intent, channel selection, read/action state, and preference signals
- attention outcomes, calibration, and attention-quality learning

### 3.2 Organizational Attention™ does not own

| Truth or behavior | Owner | Organizational Attention™ role |
|---|---|---|
| Organizational identity, business/operating model, governance constraints | Company Genome™ | Consumes strategic context and non-negotiables |
| Operating doctrine, escalation policy, decision rights | Company Operating Manual™ | Enforces declared attention/escalation policy |
| Priority™, Risk™, Escalation™, Approval™, Decision™ | Universal Decision Engine™ | Consumes governed decisions and may route them |
| Evidence corpus | Institute of Knowledge™ | References provenance and confidence |
| Cross-domain reasoning and recommendation | Studio Intelligence Layer™ | Receives evidence-backed attention candidates |
| Mission and blocker state | Mission Engine™ | Receives status/blocker candidates |
| Execution order, workflow state, retry, compensation | Workflow Engine™ | Supplies urgency/attention constraints; never executes or rewrites topology |
| Founder session context, conversational tone, local focus guard, explanation | Orb™ | Supplies plans/briefs; Orb decides session presentation within the plan |
| Spatial/executive briefing projection | Executive Headquarters™ | Supplies briefing projection; Headquarters renders it |
| Channel mechanics | Attention Delivery™ subsystem | Governs delivery as part of attention plan |

### 3.3 Orb boundary correction

Orb’s existing “attention judgment” is limited to **session-local composition**:
whether and how to present an already allocated item given the founder’s
current conversation, focus state, and tone.

Organizational Attention™ owns **company-level, cross-source attention
allocation**. Orb may defer, soften, summarize, or request reassessment within
an Attention Plan™; it may not silently promote or suppress mandatory
safety/legal/founder-policy attention.

## 4. Inputs and assessment

### 4.1 Attention inputs

Organizational Attention™ assesses candidates using:

- Company Genome™ strategy, constraints, organizational roles, commitments, and
  approved attention policy references
- Company Operating Manual™ decision rights, escalation paths, service levels,
  quiet hours, and approval requirements
- Universal Decision Engine™ priority, risk, escalation, approval, and
  confidence records
- Mission Engine™ goals, dependencies, blockers, deadlines, and outcomes
- Workflow Engine™ exception, backlog, latency, failure, and capacity signals
- finance, inventory, fulfillment, customer, contractual, compliance, and
  market/virality signals through evidence-bearing source adapters
- Studio Intelligence™ synthesized patterns, alternatives, and recommendations
- AI workforce health, delegation boundaries, confidence degradation, and
  unhandled exception signals
- founder/calendar/focus context, explicit preference, prior decisions, and
  resolved/dismissed outcome history

No raw metric, event, model prediction, or departmental request has an
automatic right to interrupt an executive.

### 4.2 Attention dimensions

| Dimension | Question answered | Must not be confused with |
|---|---|---|
| **Importance** | How much does this affect strategic goals, commitments, trust, or company survival? | How soon it happens |
| **Urgency** | How quickly does the option to act degrade or harm become irreversible? | Overall strategic value |
| **Executive necessity** | Does this require the recipient’s authority, judgment, or informed decision? | Team inconvenience |
| **Actionability** | Is there a clear next decision/action and accountable owner? | Mere information value |
| **Risk / reversibility** | What happens if delayed, ignored, or acted on incorrectly? | Notification engagement |
| **Confidence** | Is evidence sufficient for the proposed attention level? | Certainty |
| **Attention cost** | What focus, timing, and interruption burden does this impose? | Severity |
| **Routability** | Can a different authorized role, AI worker, workflow, or digest handle it? | Avoidance |

Urgency and importance remain separate canonical decision dimensions. An urgent
low-importance issue should usually route to its authorized owner. An important
non-urgent issue should enter a planned briefing, mission, or review cycle.

### 4.3 Attention classes

| Class | Meaning | Default treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **A0 — observe** | Useful signal; no action or executive context needed | Record/aggregate; no delivery |
| **A1 — digest** | Useful context with no near-term decision | Batch in scheduled briefing |
| **A2 — route** | Actionable for a delegated owner | Route to owner/AI worker; executive receives outcome only |
| **A3 — executive briefing** | Material decision or trade-off requiring executive context | Include in Orb/HQ briefing at policy-appropriate time |
| **A4 — interrupt** | Time-sensitive material risk, authority gap, or irreversible decision | Immediate, bounded, explainable interruption |

Only A4 may interrupt. Volume never promotes an item; correlated low-level
signals may become a single evidence-backed candidate.

## 5. Lifecycle and governance

```text
Source event / decision / recommendation
  -> Attention Candidate™
  -> normalize, correlate, deduplicate, and verify provenance
  -> Attention Assessment™
  -> compare against budget, policy, focus, authority, and active plans
  -> Attention Plan™
  -> observe | digest | route | briefing | interrupt
  -> delivery / Orb composition / HQ projection
  -> Attention Outcome™
  -> calibration and governed learning
```

### 5.1 Governance rules

1. A candidate must carry source, tenant, owner, confidence, time sensitivity,
   and action/decision context.
2. A4 interruption requires a stated reason, source/evidence, consequence of
   delay, intended recipient, and next action/decision.
3. Attention plans must respect Manual-defined decision rights, escalations,
   quiet/focus policy, and applicable legal/security/compliance obligations.
4. Founder override is immediate and canonical for the current plan. Durable
   preferences become learning proposals or Manual/Genome changes only through
   their governing lifecycle.
5. A dismissal is a preference signal, not proof that an underlying risk,
   contract, policy, or compliance obligation is resolved.
6. Attention policy changes that alter executive interruption rights, AI
   escalation authority, or compliance handling require Manual governance and,
   where material, Company Genome Evolution Gate™ review.

### 5.2 Dynamic and learned attention

Attention is dynamic because business state, deadlines, focus context, active
commitments, and available delegation change continuously.

It is learned only with safeguards:

- learn timing, channel, briefing depth, delegation success, and false-positive
  patterns from Attention Outcomes™
- preserve source provenance and avoid optimizing for opens, clicks, or
  dismissals alone
- do not lower a legally/policy-required escalation because it is inconvenient
- do not infer durable founder doctrine from one dismissal
- expose rationale, confidence, and override for consequential allocation

## 6. Department competition and AI escalation

### 6.1 Department competition

Departments do not compete through notification volume, dashboard placement, or
the seniority of their requester.

Their candidates are compared through:

```text
company commitment
  + strategic importance
  + urgency / irreversibility
  + risk
  + executive necessity
  + confidence
  - attention cost
  - delegability
```

The assessment must reveal trade-offs and deferred candidates. A department may
appeal a routing or classification decision through Manual-defined escalation,
but may not bypass attention budgets.

### 6.2 AI workforce escalation

An AI worker must create an Attention Candidate™ when it encounters:

- a Manual-defined escalation condition
- a required approval or missing authority
- confidence below its execution threshold
- policy, compliance, privacy, safety, or security concern
- an exception it cannot compensate within its authority envelope
- an evidence-backed material opportunity requiring a human decision

The worker must include a concise decision package: source, situation,
authority gap, risk/deadline, available options, recommendation/confidence,
and reversible next step. Organizational Attention™ routes it to the smallest
authorized recipient; founder escalation is reserved for A3/A4 or Manual policy.

## 7. Projections and consumers

### 7.1 Orb™ executive briefing

Orb receives a pinned Attention Brief™/Plan™, relevant Company Genome revision,
source citations, and founder/session focus context.

Orb must present:

1. what matters,
2. why now,
3. what changed or is at risk,
4. the decision/action required,
5. the consequence of delay,
6. confidence and alternatives,
7. what Studio OS has already delegated, suppressed, or is observing.

Orb must not dump candidate queues or convert a briefing into a notification
feed.

### 7.2 Executive Headquarters™

Executive Headquarters™ consumes an attention briefing projection. It may show
the bounded current attention set, deliberate review queue, and resolved/
delegated context. It must not become a second attention registry or a
dashboard of every underlying signal.

### 7.3 Workflow Engine™

Workflow Engine™ may consume approved priority, deadline, and attention routing
constraints to schedule queues or request human approval. It retains ownership
of execution order and state. Organizational Attention™ cannot preempt,
reorder, or execute workflow steps without Workflow/Manual authority.

### 7.4 Studio Intelligence Layer™

Studio Intelligence™ **produces and consumes** attention context but does not
own it:

- produces evidence-backed candidates, patterns, predictions, and
  recommendation alternatives
- consumes Attention Outcomes™ to calibrate its recommendations
- must not self-deliver, self-escalate, or self-authorize its recommendations

Organizational Attention™ owns cross-source allocation, timing, routing, and
delivery policy.

## 8. Inheritance and Company Genome relationship

Company Genome™ does not own Organizational Attention™. It supplies
versioned, governed context:

- strategic priorities and non-negotiables
- governance/constraint model
- organization graph and role authority
- capability/capacity semantics
- commitments and risk context

The Company Operating Manual™ supplies operating doctrine:

- escalation and approval paths
- quiet hours/focus modes and briefing cadence
- service-level and contractual response obligations
- AI worker delegation and human-review thresholds
- department decision rights

Organizational Attention™ inherits these constraints through pinned, versioned
briefs; it does not write back to the Genome or Manual. Material changes to
their attention-relevant domains invalidate affected Attention Plans™ and
require reassessment, not unreviewed mass interruption.

## 9. Scale and safety

### 9.1 As companies scale

| Company state | Attention behavior |
|---|---|
| **Founder-led** | Founder receives a small, protected A3/A4 set; most work is briefed or delegated. |
| **Functional teams** | Route to role/department owners; founder receives cross-functional, strategic, exception, and authority-gap items. |
| **Multi-layer organization** | Apply attention budgets and escalation paths per role; aggregate correlated signals into executive-level patterns. |
| **Multi-entity/global** | Tenant/entity boundaries, time zones, legal regimes, delegation chains, and local incident policies constrain routing. |

### 9.2 Safety rules

- Attention does not become an employee-surveillance score or productivity
  ranking.
- Attention budgets may suppress routine delivery, never conceal mandated legal,
  safety, security, or policy escalation.
- All candidates, assessments, plans, overrides, and outcomes are tenant-scoped
  and auditable.
- High-impact attention classification must be explainable, reviewable, and
  appealable.
- Cross-company patterns require explicit consent, anonymization, provenance,
  and governance.

---

## 10. Anti-patterns

Organizational Attention™ must never become:

- a notification feed with a more sophisticated name
- a second Priority™, Risk™, Decision™, Mission™, or Company Genome store
- a dashboard ranking every metric or department
- a mechanism for AI workers to bypass human authority
- an engagement optimization system that maximizes opens/clicks
- a hidden workflow scheduler or executive surveillance system
- a means to silently suppress compliance, safety, security, contractual, or
  policy obligations

## 11. Constitutional amendments

1. Evolve Notification Engine™ into **Organizational Attention™**, with
   Attention Delivery™ as its delivery subsystem.
2. Establish Organizational Attention™ as the sole constitutional owner of
   cross-source attention assessment, allocation, routing, timing, batching,
   budgets, delivery intent, and Attention Outcomes™.
3. Establish Attention Candidate™, Attention Assessment™, Attention Plan™,
   Attention Brief™, Attention Budget™, and Attention Outcome™ as canonical
   attention objects.
4. Preserve ownership of Company Genome, Manual, Decision Engine, Institute,
   Studio Intelligence, Mission Engine, Workflow Engine, Orb, and Headquarters
   as defined in this article.
5. Require source provenance, explicit A4 rationale, policy/authority checks,
   founder override, audit, and protected focus.
6. Require Organizational Attention™ to allocate selectively and prohibit
   notification-volume, engagement, or requester-seniority as authority.
7. Require attention-relevant Genome/Manual changes to invalidate and reassess
   affected Attention Plans™.
8. Require Studio Intelligence™ to produce evidence-backed candidates and
   consume outcomes, but prohibit it from owning allocation or self-authorizing
   delivery/escalation.

Approve Organizational Attention™ under the name proposed.

Approve it as the evolution of Notification Engine™ into attention
