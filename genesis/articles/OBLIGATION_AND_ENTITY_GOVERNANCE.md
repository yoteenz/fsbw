## 0. Constitutional decision

Studio OS shall have a universal **Obligation Model™**, but it shall **not** create a second universal identity registry.

The existing **Identity Engine™** remains the sole owner of who and what exists. Obligation & Entity Governance™ adds a constitutional relationship layer that answers:

1. What commitment exists?
2. Who is accountable for its outcome?
3. Who is legally or organizationally responsible?
4. Who may act, delegate, approve, fulfill, or terminate it?
5. What authority permits each action?
6. What happens when its owner, team, company, AI worker, or structure changes?

This is a Layer 3 Constitutional Law system. It defines truth and invariants; it does not replace the implementation responsibilities of Identity, Policy, Permission, State, Workflow, or Intelligence systems.

### 0.1 Prime directive

**No active obligation may be ownerless, unauditable, or silently stranded by organizational change.**

### 0.2 What this system is not

| Not this | It is this |
|---|---|
| A task list | A durable commitment and accountability record |
| A workflow engine | A governance layer that workflows may execute |
| A role catalog | A record of responsibility held by specific identities in a specific context |
| A permissions system | A model of what must be done; permissions decide what may be done |
| A policy engine | A model of commitments; policy determines whether their creation or fulfillment is lawful |
| A legal-advice engine | A traceable representation of declared legal commitments and required reviews |
| An HR system | A constitutional continuity model for people, teams, departments, AI, and external parties |

## 2. Participant hierarchy

### 2.1 Constitutional participant

A **Constitutional Participant™** is an existing canonical Identity that has an explicit relationship to an obligation. This is the required bridge between the Identity Engine’s narrower actor/entity distinction and the broader business question, “Who or what participates in this organization?”

Not every identity is a constitutional participant. An asset, room, document, or product becomes one only when it is a subject, beneficiary, evidence source, governed resource, or continuity anchor for a particular obligation.

### 2.2 Participant classes

| Class | Identity examples | Can hold accountable ownership | Can hold legal responsibility | Can act/fulfill | Can receive delegated authority |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Human Actor** | Founder, executive, employee, contractor | Yes | Only where legally recognized and declared | Yes | Yes |
| **Legal Entity** | Company, external organization, vendor organization, partner organization | Yes | Yes | Through authorized actors/services | Yes, through an authorized representative |
| **Organizational Entity** | Department, team, business unit | Yes, organizationally | No; requires a legal anchor | Through members/agents | Yes, within policy |
| **AI Actor** | AI Worker, AI Agent, Digital Concierge | Conditional operational ownership only | No | Yes, within authority and scope | Yes, bounded and revocable |
| **Service/System Actor** | Runtime service, integration, automated process | Conditional operational ownership only | No | Yes, within registered authority | Yes, bounded and revocable |
| **Process Entity** | Workflow, automated process | No accountable ownership | No | Executes assigned work only | No; it consumes authority from its sponsor |
| **External Actor** | Customer, individual vendor, contractor, partner | Yes where contractually declared | Only where legally recognized and declared | Yes | Yes, only within cross-organization consent |

### 2.3 Classifications required by the question

| Example | Constitutional classification | Constitutional participation |
|---|---|---|
| Founder, executive, employee, contractor | Human Actor | Full, subject to policy and authority |
| Department | Organizational Entity | May carry organizational accountability; must have a continuity route |
| AI Worker, AI Agent | AI Actor | May execute and hold bounded operational ownership; never the sole accountability or legal anchor |
| Vendor, customer, partner | External Actor or Legal Entity | May be a party, owner, fulfiller, approver, or beneficiary where explicit consent/contract exists |
| External Organization | Legal Entity | May be a contracting/owning party; cross-org governance required |
| Workflow, automated process | Process Entity | May create evidence, route, execute, and monitor; cannot be accountable owner |
| Integration, Runtime Service | Service/System Actor | May execute narrow operational duties; cannot be legal or sole accountable owner |

### 2.4 Required anchors

Every active obligation has all applicable anchors:

1. **Accountability Anchor™** — exactly one participant accountable for the outcome at any point in time.
2. **Responsibility Anchor™** — one or more participants responsible for fulfillment work.
3. **Authority Anchor™** — the permission/policy/delegation basis for a governed action.
4. **Legal Anchor™** — required for legal, contractual, regulatory, financial, privacy, employment, or customer-duty obligations; must be a human or legal entity, never an AI/process/service.
5. **Continuity Anchor™** — a role, department, company, or succession policy that identifies who inherits the obligation if the current accountable participant exits.

An AI Worker may be the Responsibility Anchor and may be the operational Accountability Anchor for low-risk internal work only when a human or legal Accountability/Legal Anchor remains named.

## 3. Obligation Model™

### 3.1 Definition

An **Obligation™** is a durable, governed commitment by one or more identified participants to produce, preserve, decide, refrain from, safeguard, or account for an outcome under declared scope, conditions, authority, and lifecycle.

It is not synonymous with the work used to satisfy it.

### 3.2 Canonical distinctions

| Concept | Question answered | Relationship to Obligation™ |
|---|---|---|
| **Task** | What discrete work should be done now? | A task may contribute evidence toward fulfillment; it does not carry accountability by itself |
| **Workflow** | In what sequence should work occur? | A workflow may implement or monitor an obligation; it is not the owner |
| **Permission** | Who may take an action here? | Enables an action; does not require that it be taken |
| **Role** | What standing function does this identity hold? | May be a continuity or authority basis; is not an active commitment |
| **Goal** | What outcome is desired? | May create obligations, but may remain aspirational without any duty |
| **Responsibility** | Who performs or contributes work? | One assignment dimension inside an obligation |
| **Ownership** | Who is accountable for the outcome/resource? | For obligations, refers to the singular Accountability Anchor |
| **Authority** | Who may decide, approve, delegate, or act? | Constrains obligation actions; authority is neither ownership nor accountability |

### 3.3 Minimum constitutional envelope

Every active Obligation™ must retain:

```yaml
obligationId: immutable canonical identifier
kind: legal | regulatory | financial | customer | operational | fiduciary | safety | privacy | employment | knowledge | governance | service
statement: specific commitment or prohibited outcome
subject: canonical object(s) affected
scope: organization | company | department | team | project | workflow | external relationship
accountabilityAnchor: one active participant
responsibilityAnchors: one or more participants
legalAnchor: required where applicable
continuityAnchor: successor rule or organizational container
authorityBasis: policy + permission + delegation/decision references
conditions: activation, deadline/cadence, evidence standard, exception rules
state: lifecycle state from §4
provenance: creating interaction, decision, contract/policy/source reference
audit: immutable history of significant actions and outcomes
```

This is a constitutional envelope, not an implementation schema. Implementations may add fields but may not omit an applicable anchor, state, provenance, or audit trail.

### 3.4 Ownerless obligations

An obligation may be drafted or proposed before acceptance. It may not be **active** without an Accountability Anchor and Continuity Anchor.

If an active obligation loses its Accountability Anchor through offboarding, deletion, revocation, reorganization, or cross-org disconnect, it enters **orphaned** status immediately. “Orphaned” is a governance violation, not a normal waiting state:

1. Freeze unauthorized delegation or termination.
2. Preserve all evidence and history.
3. Notify the Continuity Anchor and escalation route.
4. Assign a provisional accountable participant only through authorized transfer.
5. Escalate according to impact; legal/regulatory/customer/safety duties cannot be silently paused.

## 4. Obligation lifecycle

### 4.1 States

```text
draft → proposed → accepted → active → fulfillment_review → fulfilled → archived
                   ↘ declined / withdrawn
active → suspended → active
active → transfer_pending → active
active → breached → remediating → fulfilled | terminated
active → disputed → active | terminated
active → waived → archived
active → expired → archived
active → orphaned → transfer_pending | escalated
```

### 4.2 Lifecycle invariants

1. Creation identifies a proposed accountability route, source, scope, and policy basis.
2. Acceptance requires an eligible accountable participant or an authorized organizational acceptance rule.
3. Activation requires all applicable anchors and a defined evidence standard.
4. Fulfillment requires evidence, not merely workflow completion.
5. Breach records the missed/violated condition, impact, facts, and remediation owner; it never erases the original obligation.
6. Waiver or termination requires the authority mandated by policy and preserves the reason, scope, and remaining duties.
7. Transfer is a lifecycle event, not an in-place field edit.
8. Archive preserves the history and successor/supersession references.

### 4.3 Violation

A participant may violate an obligation only where the participant had a declared obligation relationship, applicable authority, and action/inaction trace. The system must distinguish:

- **Breach:** a condition of the obligation was not met.
- **Policy violation:** an action violated a governing policy, whether or not fulfillment occurred.
- **Authority violation:** an actor performed an action without valid authority.
- **Execution failure:** a workflow, service, or AI failed within its delegated scope; accountability still resolves through its human/legal anchor.

No AI output, model inference, or workflow failure may be represented as human misconduct without evidence and review.

## 5. Delegation, approval, and accountability

### 5.1 Delegation Model™

Delegation transfers a defined **authority to act**, and may assign **responsibility to fulfill**. It does not silently transfer final accountability or legal responsibility.

Every Delegation™ must state:

| Field | Constitutional requirement |
|---|---|
| Delegator | Holds current delegable authority |
| Delegate | Eligible participant with identity, scope, and required trust/credentials |
| Delegated action | Exact authority/capability and obligation relationship |
| Scope | Objects, money/risk ceiling, geography, data class, time, and workflow boundary |
| Effective period | Start, expiry, revocation conditions |
| Oversight | Accountable/Legal Anchor retained, notification, review cadence |
| Escalation | What cannot be decided or fulfilled by delegate |
| Provenance | Decision, policy, and permission references |

### 5.2 Non-delegable matters

The following cannot become solely delegated to an AI, workflow, service, or unreviewed external participant:

- Legal acceptance, legal waiver, or legal termination
- Regulatory attestations and compliance conclusions
- Fiduciary commitments or material financial commitments
- Irreversible identity, employment, privacy, or customer-rights actions
- Founder-reserved decisions
- Constitutional amendments or canon publication

Policy may designate further non-delegable obligations. Permissions cannot override such policy.

### 5.3 Accountability rule

There is one current Accountability Anchor. Shared work is permitted; shared ambiguity is not.

Multiple participants may be recorded as co-responsible, consulted, informed, beneficiaries, or approvers. If an organization needs collective accountability, a named legal/organizational body remains the singular Accountability Anchor, with its governing decision rule recorded.

### 5.4 Temporary and shared ownership

- **Temporary ownership** is a time-bounded transfer with a reversion target and escalation if expiry arrives unresolved.
- **Shared ownership** is represented by one Accountability Anchor plus multiple Responsibility Anchors and an explicit decision/approval rule.
- **Department ownership** is an organizational anchor; a department’s configured continuity/succession rule must resolve to an active accountable participant when action is required.
- **External ownership** requires consent, visibility boundaries, contract/policy basis, and a legal anchor on each required side.

### 5.5 Escalation

Escalation is mandatory when ownership is orphaned, a delegate lacks authority, a deadline/risk threshold is crossed, evidence is insufficient, a breach occurs, or a legal/safety/privacy/customer duty cannot be completed.

Escalation routes are policy-defined and impact-sensitive:

```text
Responsible participant → Accountability Anchor → organizational continuity owner
→ required approver/reviewer → Legal Anchor / founder-reserved authority
```

Attention systems may rank and route escalation. They may not suppress it, redefine its authority, or close it without the required decision.

## 6. AI responsibility doctrine

AI Workers are constitutional Actors under Identity Engine™. They may receive bounded authority and may fulfill operational obligations, provided that the following constraints all hold:

1. An AI Worker has a canonical identity, registered operator/sponsor, scope, lifecycle, capability set, and audit trail.
2. The obligation declares a human or legal Accountability/Legal Anchor for work with consequential impact.
3. Delegated authority is explicit, contextual, time-bounded, revocable, and policy-checked at execution.
4. The AI discloses its actor identity in relevant interactions and preserves inputs, decision basis, actions, confidence, and evidence.
5. The AI may recommend, execute within scope, request clarification, pause, and escalate. It may not self-expand authority.
6. The AI cannot accept legal liability, replace a required human review, waive a duty, conceal a breach, or rewrite its own audit record.
7. The AI’s operational ownership is automatically suspended when its sponsor, policy basis, credential/trust status, or runtime integrity becomes invalid.

An AI may therefore “own” an internal operational obligation only in the limited sense of being the named operational steward. It never becomes the sole constitutional answer to “Who is accountable?” for consequential work.

## 7. Inheritance, transfer, restructuring, and termination

### 7.1 Inheritance Model™

Obligations do not inherit through informal assumptions such as “the next manager owns it.” They inherit through explicit Continuity Anchors:

| Change event | Required result |
|---|---|
| Human offboarding/death/unavailability | Transfer through named role, manager, department, or succession policy; otherwise orphan + escalate |
| Department restructure | Preserve obligation IDs; map old container to successor container; re-evaluate accountable/responsible anchors |
| Company merger/split | Create a continuity decision defining retained, split, novated, terminated, or disputed obligations |
| AI retirement/suspension | Reassign operational responsibility and preserve all evidence/decision context |
| Vendor/customer/partner termination | Review contractual/customer duties; do not terminate outstanding obligations merely because the relationship closed |
| Workflow version replacement | Preserve linked obligation; attach a new fulfillment route rather than replacing history |

### 7.2 Transfer model

A valid transfer requires:

1. Initiating participant and reason;
2. policy/permission evaluation;
3. successor eligibility and acceptance where required;
4. continued Legal Anchor where applicable;
5. explicit effective timestamp;
6. preservation of the previous owner, evidence, and unresolved conditions;
7. audit interaction and, for high-impact changes, an approved decision record.

Transfer changes future accountability. It does not erase prior accountability for past acts or breaches.

### 7.3 Termination model

Only an authorized decision can terminate an active obligation. Termination records whether the obligation was fulfilled, waived, superseded, impossibly frustrated, legally released, disputed, or breached. It must name surviving duties, records, data-retention requirements, and successor obligations.

## 8. Interaction with existing constitutional systems

### 8.1 Company Genome™

Company Genome™ supplies identity, values, risk tolerance, decision principles, and approval preferences. It may shape obligation policy and escalation thresholds; it does **not** determine who has authority or mutate obligation ownership without the governing systems.

### 8.2 Workflow Engine™

Workflow Engine™ may:

- create proposals from authorized triggers,
- route acceptance and approvals,
- execute fulfillment steps,
- collect evidence,
- emit breach/escalation signals,
- report operational state.

It may not become an accountable owner, legal party, or unreviewed transfer authority. Workflow completion is evidence of execution, never automatic proof of obligation fulfillment unless the obligation defines it as sufficient evidence.

### 8.3 Organizational Attention

“Organizational Attention” is not a new authoritative registry. For this specification it means the combined routing surfaces of Orb attention, Founder Cognitive Load™, Ambient Awareness™, and notification/escalation systems.

Those systems own prioritization, interruption, batching, and presentation. Obligation & Entity Governance™ owns whether escalation exists, who must receive it, and whether it is resolved. Attention cannot silently defer a legal, safety, privacy, customer-rights, or orphaned-obligation escalation.

### 8.4 Studio Intelligence™

Studio Intelligence™ may retrieve obligation context, recommend owners, assess risk, summarize evidence, predict breach, and propose delegation. It may create an obligation only through an authorized interaction/decision path. It never infers authority from confidence, model capability, role labels, or historical behavior.

### 8.5 Company/Organization Operating Manual™

The operating manual is a human-readable, living projection of active policy, roles, obligations, delegation rules, escalation routes, and recurring duties. It does not become a competing source of truth. The Obligation Registry and governing policy/identity/decision references remain canonical.

### 8.6 Institute of Knowledge™

The constitutional **Institute of Knowledge™** reviews official doctrine, taxonomy changes, precedent, auditability, and amendments. It may validate whether a new obligation class or delegation rule is constitutionally coherent; it does not own live operational obligations.

This is distinct from **Studio Institute™**, which may teach obligation-related training and certifications but cannot confer authority unless Policy and Permission systems recognize the credential.

## 9. Governance rules

1. **Canonical reference rule:** all participants reference Identity Engine identities.
2. **Single accountability rule:** every active obligation has exactly one current Accountability Anchor.
3. **Continuity rule:** every active obligation has a Continuity Anchor.
4. **Legal separation rule:** legal responsibility cannot be assigned solely to AI, a workflow, a service, or a department without a legal entity/human anchor.
5. **Delegation separation rule:** delegation of execution/authority does not silently delegate accountability, legal duty, or founder-reserved authority.
6. **Evidence rule:** fulfillment, waiver, transfer, breach, and termination require appropriate evidence and auditable interactions.
7. **No silent restructuring rule:** structural changes cannot erase, rewrite, or strand obligations.
8. **Policy-before-permission rule:** permissions may grant capabilities only within policy; neither may bypass constitutional constraints.
9. **AI boundary rule:** AI authority is explicit, bounded, revocable, observable, and escalatable.
10. **Human override rule:** a qualified human may override automation through an auditable decision; override never erases history.
11. **Privacy and cross-org rule:** external participant obligations require consent, scope, visibility, and applicable legal/policy bases.
12. **No inferred blame rule:** a breach/violation record distinguishes facts, system failure, and human misconduct; conclusions requiring judgment receive review.

## 10. Scalability analysis

This model scales to thousands of humans and millions of AI workers because it avoids global role expansion and ownership ambiguity:

| Scaling challenge | Constitutional answer |
|---|---|
| Millions of AI Workers | AI identities are actors with bounded delegated authority; no AI becomes an unbounded legal/accountable principal |
| Frequent restructuring | Stable obligation IDs plus temporal assignment history and Continuity Anchors preserve accountability |
| Cross-company collaboration | Legal/organizational anchors, consent, policy scope, and explicit external relationships replace informal sharing |
| High automation volume | Workflows/services execute; accountability remains a compact graph of named anchors |
| Audit and incident review | Append-only interaction/decision/state history records who knew, acted, authorized, and inherited |
| Organizational memory | Obligation outcomes become evidence and learning inputs without granting memory authority over current policy |
| Query cost | Operational projections may be materialized by scope/participant/time; the canonical graph remains the source of truth |

The core design constraint is graph cardinality discipline: store durable obligations and meaningful assignment/authority edges, not a denormalized copy of every task, event, or permission check.

## 11. Constitutional risks and controls

| Risk | Constitutional control |
|---|---|
| Duplicate identity/entity models | Identity Engine remains sole identity registry; this system stores references only |
| Treating every task as an obligation | Obligation threshold requires durable commitment, anchor, lifecycle, and evidence standard |
| AI accountability theater | AI can be operational steward, never sole legal/consequential accountable principal |
| Shared ownership ambiguity | Exactly one Accountability Anchor; collaboration uses explicit responsibility roles |
| Role-based bypasses | Authority is evaluated by Permission + Policy in context, not job title |
| Silent transfer during reorg | Transfer/continuity is an auditable state transition |
| Workflow completion mistaken for fulfillment | Evidence standard is obligation-specific |
| Manual/document drift | Operating Manual is projection, not source of truth |
| Excessive governance burden | Apply obligation classification only to durable commitments with consequential or recurring impact |
| Premature legal interpretation | Model declared facts/anchors/escalations; route legal conclusions to qualified review |

## 12. Recommended Genesis amendments

These are proposed amendments, not ratified changes. They must pass Genesis amendment and Institute review before entering the frozen Master Specification.

1. **Amend Identity Engine™**  
   Add Constitutional Participant™ as a relationship projection, clarify that “Entity” retains Identity Engine meaning (non-initiating identity), and explicitly map legal entities, organizational entities, process entities, service actors, and AI actors.

2. **Amend Universal Interaction Model™**  
   Register the governed interaction family: `propose_obligation`, `accept_obligation`, `assign_responsibility`, `delegate_authority`, `transfer_obligation`, `record_fulfillment`, `record_breach`, `waive_obligation`, `terminate_obligation`, and `escalate_obligation`.

3. **Amend Universal Decision Architecture™**  
   Require explicit authority, accountability, legal, continuity, rollback, and escalation fields for decisions that create, transfer, waive, or terminate consequential obligations.

4. **Amend Policy and Permission Engine contracts**  
   Define delegation as an authorization object with scope, expiry, revocation, and audit. Establish the rule that permission grants cannot supersede non-delegable policy/constitutional duties.

5. **Amend State Engine™**  
   Register the §4 obligation lifecycle profile and orphaned-obligation escalation as a protected lifecycle transition.

6. **Create a future constitutional amendment candidate**  
   After review, ratify Obligation & Entity Governance™ as a foundational platform capability. Its Master Spec projection must cite this Genesis article and must not create competing identity, task, workflow, policy, or permission truth.

## 13. Constitutional acceptance criteria

This constitutional closure is valid when Studio OS can answer, for every active consequential obligation:

1. What exactly is promised, required, or prohibited?
2. Who is accountable right now?
3. Who is responsible for fulfillment?
4. What legal or organizational entity anchors responsibility?
5. Who may act, delegate, approve, transfer, waive, or terminate it — and why?
6. What authority and policy permit each action?
7. What evidence proves fulfillment, breach, or exception?
8. Who inherits it if the owner, AI worker, team, department, company, or workflow changes?
9. What escalation is mandatory if it becomes orphaned, overdue, disputed, or unsafe?
10. What history survives restructuring, provider replacement, automation failure, and future audit?

If Studio OS cannot answer these questions from canonical references, the obligation is constitutionally incomplete.
