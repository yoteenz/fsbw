**Project:** Studio OS

**Status:** Constitutional amendment proposal for Genesis review  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Authority:** Genesis.md  
**Extends:** Company Genome™ Living Organizational Genome, Universal Decision
Architecture™, Constitution First Principle™  
**Decision:** Approve a permanent lifecycle governance capability; do not create
independent peer engines named Evolution Gate™ or Projection Engine™.

---

## 0. Constitutional decision

Company Genome™ needs a permanent constitutional lifecycle.

The proposal is correct in intent but needs three corrections:

1. **Not every signal should pass a founder-level constitutional gate.** Raw
   observations must be able to become evidence and proposals without blocking
   learning.
2. **Not every genome change should regenerate every dependent system.** That
   would create unnecessary churn, unsafe activation, and unreproducible state.
3. **Rollback must not mutate history.** Restoring a previous organizational
   model is a new, auditable revision with selective downstream restoration.

The approved capability is **Company Genome Evolution Lifecycle™**.

It is a governed lifecycle owned by Company Genome™ and implemented through
existing constitutional capabilities: Decision Architecture™, Canon Engine™,
Genesis review, Company Operating Manual™, Workflow Engine™, and projection
consumers.

It is not a new peer source of truth.

## 1. Prime law

```text
Signals may teach the Genome.
Evidence may propose a revision.
Only a governed revision may change the active Genome.
Only an approved impact plan may activate affected projections.
History is immutable.
```

## 3. Evolution Gate™

### 3.1 Decision

Approve an **Evolution Gate™** as a lifecycle gate, not a stand-alone engine.

Its purpose is to decide whether a proposed Genome revision is:

- valid,
- sufficiently evidenced,
- internally consistent,
- authorized,
- safe to project,
- and ready to become active.

### 3.2 Required validation

Every proposal must be assessed for:

- evidence quality, provenance, recency, and scope
- internal consistency across Genome domains
- policy, compliance, privacy, rights, and tenancy implications
- ownership and authority
- dependencies and downstream projections
- activation risk and reversibility
- contradiction status
- whether founder/delegated approval is required

### 3.3 Proportional gate classes

| Class | Example | Gate behavior | Activation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G0 — observation** | New customer signal or metric | Record only; no active Genome change | Automatic evidence intake |
| **G1 — low-impact learning** | Confidence correction with no behavior/authority change | Evidence + consistency validation; delegated review permitted | Revision may activate under approved policy |
| **G2 — operational change** | Capability, department charter, service-level, or workflow-candidate change | Full impact plan + responsible owner review | Selective projection activation |
| **G3 — material organizational change** | Business model, authority, compliance, data boundary, external commitment, brand/business constraint | Full gate + founder approval | Explicit activation per projection |
| **G4 — constitutional change** | New constitutional owner, relationship, governing rule, or organization ontology | ARR / Terra constitutional review + founder approval where required | Genesis amendment before activation |

No class may bypass a required higher class by being described as a “small”
implementation adjustment.

## 4. Versioned Company Genome™

### 4.1 Immutable revisions

Approve immutable Company Genome revisions.

There is never an in-place rewrite of an active organizational genome:

```text
Genome v1.0.0
  -> proposal
  -> gate
  -> Genome v1.1.0
  -> selective projections
  -> activation
```

### 4.2 Version semantics

| Revision type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Major** | Material organizational model / authority / business architecture break | New regulated product model; new legal entity; company restructuring |
| **Minor** | Backward-compatible capability, operating-model, or governed constraint evolution | New department capability, revised service-level model |
| **Patch** | Correction or confidence/evidence update with no changed organizational behavior | Corrected market classification, strengthened evidence |

Every revision has:

- immutable `genomeId`, `revisionId`, semantic version, and snapshot hash
- parent revision and lineage
- lifecycle state: draft, proposed, approved, active, superseded, retired
- evidence package and Constitutional Diff™
- approval/decision history
- Projection Impact Plan™
- activation and restoration history

### 4.3 Active revision rule

One company may have:

- one **active** revision for normal operations,
- one or more **draft/proposed** revisions,
- pinned historic revisions for in-flight workflows, missions, and audits,
- a safe fallback revision only where policy permits.

Parallel revisions are allowed for simulation and review; they must not both
silently govern the same active decision context.

## 5. Constitutional Diff™

Approve **Constitutional Diff™** as a mandatory revision artifact.

A diff is not a textual comparison only. It explains organizational behavior.

Required content:

| Diff field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| What changed | Domain objects, relationship changes, added/removed constraints |
| Why | Evidence, proposal rationale, decision context |
| Who approved | Owner, delegated authority, founder, Terra where applicable |
| Confidence | Evidence quality, uncertainty, and unresolved assumptions |
| Behavioral change | What departments, AI workers, workflows, customer experience, or decisions will behave differently |
| Dependency impact | Affected systems and projection class |
| Policy/risk impact | Permissions, compliance, privacy, finance, external commitment, rollback implications |
| Projection plan | Retain, invalidate, regenerate draft, validate, activate, or retire |
| Compatibility | In-flight work, existing contracts, migration and staleness rules |

No material revision may be activated without a readable Constitutional Diff™.

## 6. Projection model

### 6.1 Decision

Reject a universal **Projection Engine™** as a new constitutional peer.

Approve a **Projection Planner™** as a Company Genome Evolution Lifecycle
capability.

The Planner does not own downstream systems. It computes impact from declared
relationships and emits a Projection Impact Plan™. Each consumer retains
authority to compile and validate its own projection.

### 6.2 Projection states

```text
retained
  -> stale
  -> regeneration-requested
  -> draft-projected
  -> validated
  -> approval-pending
  -> active
  -> superseded
  -> retired
```

### 6.3 Default projection policy

| Consumer | Default response to active Genome revision | Activation authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand DNA™** | Generate/review a constraint-impact brief only when business, audience, trust, category, or strategic identity inputs change | Brand owner + founder when material |
| **Experience Department DNA™** | Do not regenerate from operational changes alone; regenerate only after approved Experience/Brand/department-semantic change | Experience governance |
| **Department Operating Charter™** | Regenerate draft when capability, ownership, interface, capacity, or control changes | Department owner / operating authority |
| **Company Operating Manual™** | Mark affected sections stale; generate amendment draft, never silently rewrite active doctrine | Manual owner; founder for material doctrine |
| **Workflow Engine™** | Create migration/validation request for affected workflow definitions; never mutate active executions | Workflow owner + policy/approval gate |
| **Studio Intelligence™** | Update context index and mark recommendations stale; re-evaluate on next request | Automatic safe refresh; no action authority change |
| **Experience Engine™** | Invalidate affected compiled experience contexts; no automatic public visual change | Experience validation gate |
| **Experience Runtime™** | Retain pinned active runtime state; use new compiled context only for new/approved sessions | Runtime + experience activation gate |
| **Executive Headquarters™** | Generate capability/briefing/room composition proposal; do not silently reconfigure founder workspace | Founder / HQ authority |
| **AI Workforce™** | Recompute authority-envelope draft; suspend scope expansion until approval | Founder/delegated authority; policy gate |
| **Institute of Knowledge™** | Archive diff, evidence, approval, and revision; update references/indexes | Automatic archival; canon rules remain |

### 6.4 Automatic, selective, and approval-based work

| Projection action | Policy |
|---|---|
| Archive evidence, diff, version lineage, index references | Automatic |
| Mark stale, invalidate caches, refresh read-only reasoning context | Automatic when policy-safe |
| Generate a draft manual, charter, workflow migration, brand brief, or HQ proposal | Selective and reviewable |
| Activate changed authority, external behavior, public brand expression, workflow execution, automation, or founder workspace | Approval-based |

The default is **no automatic behavioral activation**.

## 7. Dependency regeneration

Projection Planner™ must operate on declared relationships, not on a hardcoded
list of systems.

For each affected relationship, it determines:

```text
Does this change alter:
  - organizational meaning?
  - authority or policy?
  - execution behavior?
  - public experience?
  - external commitment?
  - data/tenant/compliance boundary?
  - an in-flight operation?
```

Only then may it select retain, stale, draft regenerate, validate, seek
approval, activate, or retire.

### 7.1 In-flight work

- Workflows, missions, production packages, and runtime sessions pin the Genome
  revision that authorized them.
- A new active Genome revision governs new work by default.
- In-flight work migrates only through a dedicated compatibility/migration
  decision.
- G3/G4 safety or compliance changes may suspend affected work rather than wait
  for normal completion.

## 8. Restoration and rollback

### 8.1 Decision

Approve restoration. Reject destructive rollback.

```text
Do not mutate v3 back into v2.
Create a new v4 that restores selected meaning from v2 with a new rationale.
```

This preserves auditability, learning, approvals, and the explanation of why
the organization changed its mind.

### 8.2 Restoration process

```text
Restoration trigger
  -> identify prior revision / selected domains
  -> Constitutional Diff™ against current active revision
  -> impact and compatibility assessment
  -> Evolution Gate class
  -> new restoration revision
  -> selective projection plan
  -> validation / approval
  -> activation
```

### 8.3 Projection restoration

Restoration must not regenerate all systems by default.

It restores only projections affected by the selected domains and only after
consumer validation. Historical projections remain immutable audit artifacts.

## 9. Contradiction resolution and authority

### 9.1 Decision

Reject a single universal precedence chain.

Authority is **domain-specific**. A global hierarchy would falsely let visual
identity override policy, or workflow state override company strategy.

### 9.2 Permanent precedence rules

| Conflict | Authoritative rule | Required resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Genome organizational fact vs Brand DNA™ | Genome governs business/operating constraint; Brand governs identity/expression inside that constraint | Decision DNA™ case if brand ambition requires changing a business constraint |
| Genome operating model vs Department Operating Charter™ | Genome governs organizational topology/capability; Charter governs department-local detail | Charter amendment or Genome revision depending on scope |
| Genome constraint vs Company Operating Manual™ | Genome governs organizational applicability; Manual governs actionable doctrine | Manual must be amended or contradiction escalated; Manual cannot silently change Genome fact |
| Genome constraint vs Workflow Engine™ | Genome/Manual/Policy define allowed boundary; Workflow owns execution mechanics | Block/suspend/migrate workflow; never allow workflow to overrule policy |
| Genome claim vs Institute evidence | Institute owns evidence corpus; Genome owns evaluated organizational meaning | Evidence reassessment and Evolution Proposal; evidence does not auto-rewrite active Genome |
| Genome vs Studio Intelligence recommendation | Genome provides authoritative context; Intelligence provides non-authoritative reasoning | Recommendation must surface conflict and confidence; cannot mutate Genome |
| Genome vs Experience/Runtime projection | Genome owns organizational semantics; Experience owns expression/runtime state | Mark projection stale; compile/validate new projection before activation |
| Genome revision vs Constitution | Genesis constitutional law governs all Genome evolution | G4 ARR / Terra review and amendment required |

### 9.3 Higher-order constraints

Regardless of domain, the following always constrain a Genome change:

1. Constitutional law and founder authority
2. Applicable law, compliance, privacy, rights, and security policy
3. Explicit approved organizational commitments
4. Evidence quality and declared uncertainty
5. Tenant isolation and consent boundaries

## 10. Long-term scalability

### 10.1 Multi-company operation

- Every revision, evidence package, diff, projection plan, and restoration is
  tenant-scoped.
- Shared templates are schemas, not shared active organizational meaning.
- Cross-company learning requires explicit consent, anonymization, provenance,
  and policy approval.

### 10.2 Performance and operational scale

- Store immutable revision manifests and semantic deltas, not full copies of
  high-volume operational records.
- Use snapshot hashes, dependency indexes, and projection caches.
- Recompute only affected projections.
- Preserve in-flight pinned revisions for audit and safe completion.
- Retire projections only under declared retention and legal-hold policies.

### 10.3 Governance scale

- Gate class is determined by impact, not by which team proposes the change.
- Delegated authority may approve only within explicit decision rights.
- High-impact changes remain founder-reviewable.
- G4 changes always return through Constitution First Principle™ and ARR.

## 11. Anti-patterns

Company Genome Evolution Lifecycle™ must never become:

- a founder-approval bottleneck for harmless observations
- automatic regeneration of every downstream system
- a destructive database rollback mechanism
- a universal precedence ladder
- a second Workflow Engine™, Canon Engine™, or Genesis compiler
- a way to silently expand AI authority
- a mutable history without lineage, diff, evidence, or approval
- a “sync all” button that activates public or operational behavior without
  validation

## 12. Constitutional amendments

1. Establish Company Genome Evolution Lifecycle™ as a permanent capability of
   Company Genome™, not a peer engine.
2. Establish Evolution Gate™, immutable Genome revisions, Constitutional Diff™,
   Projection Impact Plan™, and Restoration Proposal™ as canonical lifecycle
   objects.
3. Establish Projection Planner™ as an impact-planning capability. Consumer
   systems retain compilation, validation, and activation authority.
4. Require every material Genome revision to have a version, evidence package,
   diff, approval record, impact plan, and pinned projection lineage.
5. Require selective regeneration and prohibit silent behavioral activation.
6. Require forward-only restoration revisions and prohibit destructive rollback.
7. Establish domain-specific precedence rules and contradiction cases.
8. Require ARR/Terra review for G4 constitutional evolution and founder review
   for G3 material organizational changes.
